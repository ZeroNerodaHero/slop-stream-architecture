# screen

built. the screen share, and the frame around it: sources in a sidebar, layers over the preview, presets, a mixer, and mic only (the mic alone, the avatar for a face). the obs idea, as numbers. obs itself waits.

## what it is for

a site with nothing to connect a stream to. the person is already on that page, goes live here, and shares that tab. watchers get the tab big and the face over it, where the streamer put it. a second screen, a window, a picture, a gif, a border around the face, or their avatar moving with their voice instead of a face: all layers of the one frame. the page does not composite them: every picture leaves the browser on its own track, and every player lays them out the same way from a few numbers in the slots frame. text stays sharp, nothing is drawn on a canvas, and the streamer can be in another tab the whole time (a hidden tab stops painting; capture, encoding and web audio go on).

the dummies are untouched. they send one camera and no screen, say nothing about a scene, and watchers of a dummy see what they always saw.

## sources

`infra/scene.ts`, the list `publish-atoms.ts` keeps as `sourcesAtom`: the camera (there from the moment it is on), each shared screen, each picture, the avatar. bottom first; a source on the stream is a layer of the scene, in that order. a source off the stream keeps its box, so the eye brings it back where it was; a video source off the stream is a tile for watchers (what every screen used to be), a picture or the avatar off the stream is only in the list.

the sidebar (`live/Sources.tsx`, `#sources`, top of the stack first, the way a layers panel reads): a row per source with a little picture of it, the eye (`#source-hide-<id>`), up and down the stack (`#source-up-`, `#source-down-`), `fill` or `whole` on a video (`#source-fit-`), `fit to the camera` on a picture (`#source-fit-camera-`: the same box, just over it, which is how a border lands on the face), and `×` (`#source-remove-`: a screen stops sharing, a picture or the avatar leaves the scene, the camera is turned off, see mic only). the `+` (`#add-source`): `camera` while it is off (`#add-camera`), `avatar` (`#add-avatar`, one), `screen or window`, `image or gif`, and under those the pictures this name has put up before. before start or live, any of them: the frame can be laid out before anyone sees it. ids: `cam`, `s1`..`s3` (the wire slot), `i<n>`, `me`. the panel's one line of words (`#source-status`): a picture going up or refused, a camera asked for and refused.

## mic only

the avatar: the streamer's profile picture (the one the profile tab saves; the letter the name makes when there is none, `GeneratedAvatar`), a square in the middle of its box, moving with the voice the way a png avatar does: a nod up and a little bigger with every word, the border blue while they talk (`AvatarLayer.tsx`; someone who asked for less motion gets the border alone). nothing of it is sent: every player draws it from the stream's name, and what moves it is the sound each side already has, the mic on the go live page (the mixer's own reading once that is up, the way the mic chip does) and the opus arriving on the watcher's `<video>` (an `AnalyserNode` on that track, `mic-level.ts`). so it costs no bits, no encode, and it keeps moving while the streamer is in another tab. a reader made on a watcher's page with no click behind it sits suspended until the first click or key anywhere on the page (`resume`), the unmute being the one that usually comes.

the camera's `×` is mic only: the picture's track is stopped (the light goes off), the mic stays, the camera leaves the scene and the avatar stands in for the face, filling the frame when it is all there is (`cameraOff`). live, the camera's section goes quiet (`replaceTrack(null)`) and the slots frame names no slot 0. kept (`settings.micOnly`): the next visit opens the mic alone and never asks for the camera (`startMic`; status `mic ready`, `mic blocked`, `no mic`). the `+`'s `camera` turns it on again, before start or live (`cameraOn`: the track into the mic's stream and, live, into the camera's section, which was offered empty for exactly this); the camera is laid out where a camera goes and the avatar goes off the stream, its box kept, so the eye brings it back beside the camera. a camera refused or missing on arrival offers the way out under the empty list (`#mic-only`, `go mic only`); a mic refused with mic only kept offers the camera (`#use-camera`). not without a mic: the camera's `×` is disabled then, since nothing would be left to stream.

in the rules and the presets the avatar is the face when there is no camera: it takes the camera's role (`roleOf`), so `face` and `screen + face` and the first share's arrangement lay a mic-only stream out like any other, and `settings.lastArrangement` is where the face goes, camera or avatar. beside a camera it has no role: it arrives centred like a picture, keeps its place through a preset like one, and the camera's `fit to the camera` does not know it. one avatar: it is the streamer's own picture.

## the share

`screen or window` runs `getDisplayMedia` (`shareScreen`). the browser's picker: a screen, a window, or a tab. the page caps it at 1920×1080 and 30 fps (`SCREEN_CAP`), sets `contentHint` to `detail`, and sends no audio. live, `replaceTrack` fills the first empty screen section; before start the track waits in its slot and the section is offered with it in (`goLive`). those sections are in the offer from the start (`SCREEN_SLOTS` = 3), so a share mid-stream renegotiates nothing. this site's own tab is kept out of the picker (`selfBrowserSurface: "exclude"`, sharing it is a mirror), and the tab can be switched without stopping (`surfaceSwitching: "include"`). a browser that throws on the hints is asked again with the cap alone (`pickDisplay`); the picker closed without a choice is nothing, and no second picker. the screens go with the stream when it stops; the camera, the pictures and the avatar stay.

the box copies the packets. vp8 only (`media.rs`). a retina capture scaled down inside the encoder would spend the bits on pixels the cap cannot afford (`bitrates.md`), so the capture cap stays 1920×1080.

## the scene

one record, `Scene` in `relay.ts`, bottom layer first:

```
{ "layers": [
  { "id": "s1",  "kind": "screen", "slot": 1, "x": 0, "y": 0, "w": 1, "h": 1 },
  { "id": "cam", "kind": "camera", "slot": 0, "x": 0.73, "y": 0.714, "w": 0.25, "h": 0.25 },
  { "id": "i1",  "kind": "image",  "asset": 12, "x": 0.35, "y": 0.322, "w": 0.3, "h": 0.356 },
  { "id": "me",  "kind": "avatar", "x": 0.02, "y": 0.631, "w": 0.333, "h": 0.333 } ] }
```

a box is its top left corner and its size, each a fraction of the 16:9 frame, to thousandths (`clampBox`), so the go live page's preview and every watcher's player draw the same picture at any size. a video layer is one of the publisher's slots and draws with `object-fit` (`fit`: a camera fills its box, `cover`, so resizing it is cropping; a screen shows the whole picture, `contain`, so text is never cut; the row's `fill`/`whole` flips it). a picture is an api asset by id, drawn `fill`: its box keeps the picture's own shape (`ratio`, from the decoded size), so the box is the picture. the avatar carries nothing but its box (the watcher has the name, and the sound): a square in the middle of it, the box's shorter side, on no ground, so in a corner over a screen the picture alone floats there; its box behaves like the camera's, a shape kept through a resize and freed with shift. at most `MAX_LAYERS` 16, each at least `LAYER_MIN` 5% of the frame, inside it.

the editor is the preview (`SceneView`, the same component the watch page draws with). a layer clicked is selected (its row lights too); dragged it moves; a corner pulled resizes it with its shape kept (shift frees a video's); the arrow keys nudge 1% (shift: 5%), `[` and `]` move it through the stack, delete takes a picture out and puts a video source beside, escape lets go. the preview's own `<video>` (`#preview`) stands in for the bottom video layer; the rest are muted copies and `<img>`s.

the rules (`arrangedAfterAdd`, `arrangedAfterRemove`): one picture on its own fills the frame. the first screen beside the camera makes `screen + face` (the screen the frame, the camera bottom right a quarter wide), or the arrangement this browser last had for the two (`settings.lastArrangement`). a third picture takes a corner of its own and moves nothing else: the streamer is in another tab picking the share, and a reflow then is a jump nobody asked for. a source gone leaves the rest where it is, unless one is left, which fills the frame. a picture arrives centred, 30% wide, on top.

presets (`live/Presets.tsx`, `#presets`) are geometry by role, so one applies to whatever sources there are: `face`, `screen`, `screen + face`, `face + screen`, `side by side`, `three up` (`PRESETS`), and the ones this browser saved (`save as preset`, `settings.scenePresets`, 12 at most). a preset's video layers replace the ones there, in its order; a role with no source is skipped and the chip is dimmed; a video source it does not list goes beside; pictures keep their boxes and their place (under the lowest video or above it).

the scene travels with the slots frame, nowhere else: `{"slots":[...],"scene":{...}}` on the publisher's socket when a video slot comes up or goes quiet (a screen, the camera), once the stream is up (a scene laid out before start, a picture, the camera's box, reaches the first watcher), and within `SCENE_SEND_MS` 150 ms of any other change with the latest by then (a drag is many changes a second; the box and every watcher get a few). the slots name what is live: slot 0 only while the camera is on, so a mic-only stream names none and its camera section stays quiet. the box checks the scene (`Scene::well_formed`: a short plain id, a known kind, a video slot of that publisher's own offer, an asset id for a picture, nothing more for the avatar, fractions in 0..1, 16 layers at most; a frame that fails is ignored and logged, like a bad slot) and passes it on as it came, in every `{"event":"slots"}`: right after a watcher's answer, so a late watcher has the scene before the first frame, and on every change. a publisher that never sent one (the dummy, an older page) has no scene, and the watcher falls back to the rule it always had: the first screen big, the camera a tile. an older page that does not know the avatar drops that layer and plays the sound over black.

on the watch page (`watch-atoms.ts`): the big `<video>` (`#video`, the one with the sound) stands in for the scene's bottom video layer, the others are drawn over it (`watchLayersAtom`), and every live slot in no layer is a tile. a video layer clicked is shown alone (`chosenSlotAtom`): the frame is set aside, the other live slots are tiles, and the streamer's own slot in the tiles puts the frame back (`the stream`, `#tile-scene`, when no slot of theirs can). a scene of pictures alone, or the avatar alone (mic only), plays the sound with no big picture: `watching` once the sound plays. the corner players draw the same frame, the watcher's and the broadcast's own (there the preview's `<video>` stands in for the bottom video layer, and a mic-only stream shows its avatar instead of a black box); in a player's own window, whose shape is the os's, the plain picture shows instead.

## sound

`infra/mixer.ts`, the part of `audio.md` that is built: one `AudioContext` from the start click, the mic through a fader (`level³`: obs's cubic, so half way is -18 dB and not already silence), a mute and an analyser, summed through a master fader and a limiter into a `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode` whose track is the audio section's for the whole stream. so a sound can join or leave the mix and nothing is renegotiated; a stream started without a mic still has its audio section (an empty one is offered). a shared screen brings no sound (`audio: false`; a tab's sound is `audio.md`, still a plan). nothing reaches `ctx.destination`: the streamer hears nothing of it.

the mixer (`live/Mixer.tsx`, `#mixer`, while the stream is up): a row per source (`#mix-<id>`: a bar painted from a frame loop, `#level-<id>`, with `aria-valuenow`; a mute, `#mute-<id>`; a fader, `#gain-<id>`) and `everyone hears` (`#mix-master`, the same, with a clip light, `#clip`, red for a second after a hot sample). the mic's and the master's faders are kept (`settings.mixer`). the mic chip on the preview (`#live-mic`) reads the mixer's own analyser while it is up, so the mic is never opened twice. a context the browser paused (made outside a click, interrupted) says so in red with a `sound on` button: the stream is silent until then.

## what it costs

a kilobyte or so in the slots frame. the wire otherwise follows the scene (`tuneSenders`): the stage, the biggest picture on the stream, gets the budget: a screen there has the screens' 3 Mbps less 500 kbps for each other screen, the camera there its full 2.5. the camera in a corner is sent as small as its box (`scaleResolutionDownBy` in steps, so a drag does not cost a keyframe per pixel) with the bits that is worth, 150 to 500 kbps; off the stream, small too. other screens get 500 kbps at 15 fps: readable, stuttery on a video. the encoders follow the scene `TUNE_MS` 500 ms after it settles. a screen sender keeps `degradationPreference: "maintain-resolution"` while it has a track: a page is letters and ui, so a tight uplink costs frames, not pixels. the camera is left to the browser's default, so a face keeps its frames.

the layers are css on the watcher's side: absolutely positioned boxes, no second decode, no canvas. a picture is an `<img>` from the api, cached for good (an asset's id is never reused); the avatar is the profile picture's `<img>` and one `AnalyserNode` on the sound already playing. the cpu is one vp8 encode per picture, in software, on the streamer's machine; a tab that sits still is cheap, a tab with a video in it about a core; a mic-only stream encodes nothing but opus. the box still does not decode.

## the pictures

`PUT /api/streamers/:name/assets`, the bytes as the page sends them: png, jpeg, gif or webp by the first bytes (never svg: a script), 2 mb at most, 24 a name, the same gate as the thumbnails (`may_write`). the page sends the part of the file picked in the crop dialog (`spec.md`, "the crop dialog"; `infra/picture.ts`), at the picture's own pixels and in its own format, so a png keeps its holes; the whole picture is the file itself, untouched, and a gif is always that, since a cut would be one frame of it. `stream_assets` (`migrate-9.sql`), keyed by name like `stream_thumbs` and renamed with it; the bytes at `assets/<id>/<n>.<ext>`. served back with the sniffed type, `nosniff`, and a year's cache. `GET` lists them, `DELETE /:id` removes one. the scene names a picture by id and the watcher builds the url under the stream's name, so nobody can point a layer at somebody else's picture.

## pages

go live: `#scene` the frame, `#layer-<id>` the boxes (`data-kind`, `data-slot`, the inline style their place; `#layer-<id>-video`, `#layer-<id>-img`, `#layer-<id>-avatar` with `data-voice` 0..100 and `data-speaking` while they talk; `#layer-<id>-resize-<nw|ne|sw|se>` on the selected one), `#preview` in the bottom video layer; `#sources` with its rows and `#add-source`'s menu (`#add-camera`, `#add-avatar`, `#add-screen`, `#add-image` and `#image-file`, `#library-<asset>`), `#source-status`, and under an empty list `#mic-only` or `#use-camera`; `#presets` with `#preset-<name>` (`screen + face` is `preset-screen-face`), `#save-preset`, `#preset-name`, `#preset-save`; `#mixer`; `#share-screen` beside stop, the same as `#add-screen`.

watch: `#scene`, `#layer-<id>` (a video layer a button, "show camera big"; `#layer-<id>-avatar` the same as the go live page's), `#video` in the bottom video layer, `#tiles` with `#tile-<slot>` and `#tile-scene`. the corner players: `#pip-scene`, `#live-pip-scene`.

`test/screen.js` is the check: the sources and the camera layer before start, the mixer with the stream; a screen from the `+` makes the frame on both sides; a drag and a corner follow to the watcher and to localstorage; a png is a layer with its own shape and no new stream; the order follows the arrows; the eye makes a tile and brings the layer back; a saved preset and `face + screen`; the mixer's bars move, a fader at 0 and a mute flatten the master; a second screen takes its corner and hidden is a tile; a late watcher gets it all at once; a screen's `×`; stop leaves the sources and fills the frame with the camera; then mic only: the camera's `×` leaves the mic alone with the avatar filling the frame and `settings.micOnly` kept, the stream started again is `watching` on the sound with no video coming and the avatar moving on both sides, and the `+`'s `camera` puts the camera back live with the avatar off the stream. chrome picks the screen for that test.

## not here

- obs, rtmp, or a second ingest. `spec.md` already leaves those out. a later obs door only pays off if the box forwards obs's own encode. running it back through vp8 at 3 Mbps spends a core and throws the sharper picture away.
- a newer codec. vp9 or av1 hold text in fewer bits, and `media.rs` is opus + vp8 so every watcher already knows the bytes. a second codec is its own build.
- 60 fps, 1440, 4k, or a bitrate above 3 Mbps for the screens.
- a "this is a game" switch. one mode: a page. motion at this cap is the weak spot on purpose.
- the box making a small copy of the screen. `bitrates.md` makes 720 and 360 from the camera, which is the home card. a 360 of a webpage cannot be read, so the watch page keeps the original screen packets.
- burning the layers into one picture. they stay separate; the scene is numbers, and a watcher can still put any one of them big.
- a tab's sound. only chromium hands it over, and it was a source of confusion next to a share that never has sound; the mixer takes a row for it the day `audio.md` builds it.
- scenes to switch between, transitions, text and colour sources, crops, rotation, groups that move together. a border follows the camera by `fit to the camera` again, not by a link.
- the scene as the home card or the thumbnail. `thumbs.rs` stays a frame of the camera, hidden or not. a mic-only stream has no camera frame: its card on home shows the thumbnails the streamer put up, and with none nothing yet (the avatar there is `audio.md`'s plan).
- a picture of the streamer's choosing as the avatar, or a second one for the mouth open. the avatar is the profile picture; a png put up as a picture does not move.
- a watcher asking the box for fewer slots. four decodes on a phone is heavy; a `want` frame per watcher is the fix, later.
