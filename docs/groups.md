# groups

a login joins a group, and its chat lines wear the group's badge: a small box in the group's colour with a word in capitals, next to the name, the way discord tags a bot. one group is built in and automatic, `bot`, and the chat panel can hide its lines.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  login[a login] -->|join, leave, make| groups[(groups, memberships)]
  chat[chat] -->|at hello: the badges of this name| groups
  chat -->|line: from, text, groups| panel[chat panel]
  bot[a bot's socket] -->|"hello … bot: true"| chat
  panel -->|hide bots| hidden[its lines off the screen]
```

## what for

- the fake streams (`DUMMY_STREAMS`) have pretend chatters so the panel has something to show. they are there to prove the chat works, not to be read. with a badge on them they can be told apart, and with the switch they can be put away. when real people are the chat, the switch is the thing to drop, not the badge.
- a few people who want to be seen together: a group with a badge, a picture and a banner. anyone with an account can make one and anyone can join. the maker is the owner, and a login owns a couple at most: `GROUPS_PER_OWNER` in `.env`, 2 unless set.

## the rules

- a group: the words they type (`title`, the name rule, `a-z 0-9 _ -`, 1 to 32) and four random digits (`tag`, `0000` to `9999`). two groups can share the words. the address (`name`, the url, the chat room) is the words, cut at 27 so `-` and the digits fit, then `-` and the digits (`gaming-0042`). a group made before this has no title and no tag, and the name is the words. a `badge` (1 to 12 characters, cleaned like a chat line, shown in capitals), a `color` (`#rrggbb`), a line `about` it (up to 200). made by a login, who is in it from the start.
- a login owns at most `GROUPS_PER_OWNER` groups (`.env`, read by the api at boot; empty is 2, anything under 1 is said in the log and 2 is used), the ones it made and the ones it was named owner of, and is in up to 8: every badge goes on every line it says. one more past the cap needs one deleted, or handed to another owner and stepped down from; the pages offer `create` only under the cap, which `GET /api/explore` says (`groups_per_owner`) beside the servers it counts (`owner` in `yours`).
- joining and leaving are a login's own choice. no invitations, no approval. a ban stops a join until it is lifted.
- the maker is an owner, and starts with every power. an owner can name another owner, and take it back. nobody else can, not a site admin. a member who owns as many groups as the cap allows cannot be named. a group keeps one owner while anyone else is in it: the last owner cannot leave or be removed until they name another. the last person out deletes the group.
- a custom role is a name (1 to 32, cleaned like a chat line, not the word `owner`), a colour and any of five powers: appearance (the about line, public or private, the member order), roles (make and hand out roles, never owner), channels, kick, ban. twenty roles at most. a role is known by a uuid postgres made (`ids.md`; migrate-23), not by its name: renaming one is a save of the same role, whoever holds it keeps it, and a page with the role open keeps it open under a change that arrives meanwhile. a role cannot touch an owner: only an owner gives one a role or takes one back. a role reaches only the powers its holder has: whoever makes, changes, deletes, gives or takes a role must hold every power on it (an owner and a site admin hold every one), so the roles power alone cannot write itself a ban.
- the owner role is not a row of `roles`: owning is `memberships.owner`, and the role is a word on the group, `owner_role` (migrate-27), `owner` until an owner renames it (`PUT .../owner-role`). the roles tab lists it first, a crown and the name, every power, seeing every channel, with nothing to change but the name; a crown stands for an owner on the roster, the members tab and the header, the name its tooltip. a custom role cannot take the owner role's name (400 `that name is reserved`), nor the owner role a custom role's (409).
- a role has a cooldown (`roles.cooldown`, seconds, migrate-28), off (0) unless the roles tab says otherwise, up to an hour: its holders wait that long between lines in the group's channels, whichever channel. a member with several roles waits the longest of them; the owners and a site admin wait for nobody. the chat keeps the wait as a key in redis (`cd:<server>:<account>`, alive the cooldown long, set as a line goes in) and refuses a line inside it with `cooldown` and `wait`, the ms left, the `slow down` frame's shape, so the send button counts it down (`chat.md`).
- a member can be timed out (`timeouts (group_name, user_id, until, by_id)`, migrate-28): no line of theirs in the group's channels until `until`; the chat refuses one with `timed out` and `wait`, the ms left. the kick power (`POST .../timeout`, `{ name, seconds }`, ten seconds to a week; an owner only by an owner, as a kick; not yourself), lifted early by the same (`DELETE .../timeout/:name`). a row of its own, not a column of memberships, so leaving and joining again does not end it; an expired row is nothing. the roster carries `timeout` (unix seconds, while it runs); the `timeout` fact moves it (`until`, or null when lifted).
- a channel can be kept to roles (`channel_roles`, migrate-26): a role carries the channels kept to it (`channels`, by name), and a channel kept to one or more roles is seen by the holders of those roles, the owners, the channels power and a site admin, and by nobody else: not in the room's list, the explorer, `GET .../messages`, the chat sockets (the server's socket does not follow it, and a say in it is `say which channel`), the history routes (`no such channel`, like one that is not there) or the inbox (no `chat` row for a member who does not see it). a channel no role keeps is everyone's. `general` is never kept (400). the roles power sets a role's channels from the ones it sees itself (a hidden one is `no such channel` to it, so it cannot unlock for itself what it cannot see; one already on the role that it cannot see stays), and the chat reads what a login sees as its socket opens: a role given or taken reaches a socket that is up on its next connect, which the page does on its own.
- the picture and the banner are the owner's alone (and a site admin's). no role carries them, and there is no remove: a group with a picture keeps one, or changes it for another.
- an owner, the one who made a group, or an admin from `ADMIN_NAMES` deletes it. its members are out with it.
- `bot` is built in: `BOT`, discord's blurple. nobody joins it (403) and nobody deletes it. a chat socket whose `hello` says `"bot": true` wears it, whoever it is. anyone may say so; it only ever costs the speaker, since the panel can hide the line, so there is nothing to check. the dummy's chatters say it, the pretend viewer and the pretend streamer both.
- a socket's badges are read once, at the hello: the account's groups when the line is really the account's (`verified`: the login behind the socket is the name on the line, so a streamer who went live under a free name while logged in as somebody else wears nothing), plus `bot` when asked for. a group joined mid-chat shows after a reconnect; the pages reconnect the open chat on their own after every join, leave, make and delete.
- the badges travel on the line (`groups: [{ name, badge, color }]`), kept in the redis stream with the line (the `g` field, json; absent when there are none). a room is drawn from its lines alone, no lookup. a line keeps the badges as they were when it was said.
- a group is public unless the maker says private, on the create form or later. a public one is on the lists. a private one is on the lists only for someone in it: home, `/groups`, and `GET /api/groups`. the page is still there for anyone who has the name, and a login can still join it. the owner (appearance) switches it under `manage` on the group (`listing`), and the maker can from the groups tab.
- which groups a stranger may see, who made them and how many are in each is public. the roster is for members (and a site admin), on the right of the room: each name, whether they are an owner, which roles they hold, and whether they are live. owners come first, then name, newest, or oldest, which appearance sets. a guest does not get that list. a member who is live is also named on the group's page and in `live` on the explorer, because that face is the room.
- a room is loaded once and then pushed to. arriving on `/groups/<name>` asks the api twice, in parallel: the room (`GET /api/groups/:group`, which carries the channels, who is live, and the roster and the bans this login may see) and the last 100 lines of every channel (`GET .../messages`). from then on the events socket (`status.md`) is the only source: the page watches `group:<name>` and every change to the group arrives as a `group` fact (someone joined or left, was kicked or banned, a channel was added or deleted, the about, the pictures, public or private, the member order, a role made, changed, deleted, given or taken, an owner named or unnamed, a member live or ended, the group deleted) and lands in place. changing channels asks nothing of anyone: the chat is one socket for the whole server (`chat.md`, `/chat/<server>/*`), every channel's lines on it, and the channel list only changes what is shown. the two pulls behind the push: a change about the login itself (its own membership, powers or roles) asks the room again, since what it may see depends on them, as does a role's channels changing or a role that kept channels going (a channel may have come into view or gone out of it: one that came is followed on the chat socket and `GET .../messages` is asked again for its lines, one that went takes its lines with it and the chat socket is opened again, since the chat joins what the login sees as the socket opens; `reconcileChannels` in `room-atoms.ts`); and the socket coming back after a gap asks again, since the facts of the gap are gone.

## api

|        | route                      | does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| GET    | `/api/groups`              | `[{ name, badge, color, about, made_by, members, mine, public, title, tag, automatic }]`, `bot` first, then oldest first. a private group is listed only for someone in it. `mine` is for the login cookie, always false for nobody. `made_by` is the maker's name as it is now (by id, `ids.md`: a renamed maker reads right), null for `bot`. `title` is the words, `tag` the four digits; both null for `bot` and for a group made before the digits. `name` is the address |
| POST   | `/api/groups`              | `{ name, badge, color, about?, public? }` → 201 with the group as the list shows it; the maker is in it, as its owner. `name` in the body is the title. `name` in the answer is the address (`title` plus `-` and `tag`). the same title again is another group. `public` defaults to true. 401 nobody, 400 `bad name…` / `badge: 1 to 12 characters` / `color: #rrggbb`, 403 `you own 2 groups already. that is the cap…` at `GROUPS_PER_OWNER` (`you own a group already. one each…` when it is 1) or past 8 joined, 409 `name taken` (`bot`, or eight draws of the digits all taken) |
| POST   | `/api/groups/:group/join`  | 204, also when already in it. 401 nobody, 404 `no such group`, 403 for `bot`, for `you are banned`, and past 8 groups                                                                                                                                                    |
| POST   | `/api/groups/:group/leave` | 204, also when not in it. 401 nobody. 403 `name another owner first` when this login is the only owner and someone else is still in it. the last person out deletes the group |
| DELETE | `/api/groups/:group`       | 204 for an owner, the maker, or an admin. 401 nobody, 403 `not your group` and for `bot`, 404                                                                                                                                                                            |

chat (`chat.md`): the `hello` takes `"bot": true`; every `history` and `message` line carries `groups`, an array, empty for most lines.

### the facts

every change to a group is one `group` entry on the `events` stream (`presence::publish_group`), written after the row is, with `name` the group, `what` the kind, and `data` the change as json. the gateway forwards it whole, as `{"type":"group","group":"<name>","what":"<what>", …data}`, to the sockets watching `group:<name>`, and knows nothing more about it: a new kind of change is the api's alone. `live` and `ended` for a member are the gateway's own: on a `live` or `ended` fact it looks up the name's groups (only when some socket watches some group) and tells each room's watchers `{"type":"group","group":…,"what":"live","who":"<name>"}`.

| `what` | data | from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `joined` | `who`, `avatar`, `joined` (unix seconds), `members` (the count after) | `POST .../join` |
| `left` | `who`, `by` (`leave` or `kick`), `members` | `POST .../leave`, `POST .../kick` |
| `banned` | `who`, `reason`, `members` | `POST .../ban` |
| `unbanned` | `who` | `DELETE .../bans/:name` |
| `channel` | `channel: { name, about, made_by }` | `POST .../channels` |
| `unchannel` | `channel` (the name) | `DELETE .../channels/:channel` |
| `channel_renamed` | `channel` (the old name), `name` (the new) | `PUT .../channels/:channel` |
| `channel_order` | `order`: every channel's name, top to bottom | `PUT .../channels` |
| `about` | `about` | `PUT .../about` |
| `access` | `public` | `PUT .../access` |
| `order` | `order` | `PUT .../order` |
| `owner_role` | `name`: what the owner role is called now | `PUT .../owner-role` |
| `icon`, `banner` | `icon` / `banner`: unix seconds, the new version | `PUT .../icon`, `.../banner` |
| `role` | `role: { id, name, color, perms, channels, cooldown }`, made or changed (`id` a uuid; a rename is this, with the id as it was; `channels` the ones kept to it; `cooldown` seconds, 0 none) | `POST .../roles`, `PUT .../roles/:id` |
| `timeout` | `who`, `until` (unix seconds; null when lifted) | `POST .../timeout`, `DELETE .../timeout/:name` |
| `unrole` | `id` | `DELETE .../roles/:id` |
| `held`, `unheld` | `who`, and `role: { id, name, color }` or `id` | `POST .../roles/:id/members`, `DELETE .../members/:name` |
| `owner` | `who`, `owner` (true or false) | `POST .../owners`, `DELETE .../owners/:name`, `POST .../transfer` (two: the new owner's, then the old one's) |
| `live`, `ended` | `who` | the gateway, from the stream's own facts |
| `deleted` | nothing | `DELETE /api/groups/:group`, and the last person out |

`members` rides on every fact that changes it, so a room never counts on its own. the page patches what it holds from these; the roster, the bans and its own powers are the one thing a frame cannot carry per viewer, so a fact about the login itself (`joined`, `left`, `banned`, `held`, `unheld`, `owner` with `who` the login; `role` and `unrole` for a role it holds) makes the page ask `GET /api/groups/:group` again.

## database

```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS groups (
  name text PRIMARY KEY,
  badge text NOT NULL,
  color text NOT NULL,
  about text NOT NULL DEFAULT '',
  created_by text REFERENCES streamers(name) ON DELETE SET NULL,   -- null: built in. since ids.md: created_by_id uuid, the key; this column one release more
  created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS memberships (
  name text NOT NULL REFERENCES streamers(name) ON DELETE CASCADE,  -- since ids.md: user_id uuid is the key; this column one release more
  group_name text NOT NULL REFERENCES groups(name) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  joined_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  PRIMARY KEY (name, group_name)
);
INSERT INTO groups (name, badge, color, about) VALUES ('bot', 'BOT', '#5865f2', '…') ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING;
```

## pages

- chat: every line wears its speaker's badges after the name, before the `streamer` mark. the header has a robot button, `hide bots`: on, the bots' lines are off the screen and it reads `N hidden`; a room with nothing but bots says so instead of `nothing yet`. a setting of this browser (`settings.hideBots`, localstorage), so it holds from room to room and across reloads; the chat tab of the user modal has the same switch as two cards, `bots`.
- the user modal, `groups` tab (between profile and security): `your badges` (the name and what it wears), and the list (badge, name, members, who made it, about; `join` or `leave`, `automatic` on `bot`, `make public` / `make private` and `delete`, which asks in a dialog (`#group-delete-<name>-confirm`, yes or no), for the maker). making one is not here: the tab points to `/groups` (`#group-make-there`, which closes the modal), where the dialog below takes the banner and the picture too. without a login the list shows and the rest points to the security tab.
- the profile tab's card wears the badges too, each a way to the groups tab.
- the sidebar (`follows.md`) lists the groups you are in under the group icon, each as its square with the number of members live on its corner, the row a way to the group's page; the sidebar tab of the user modal says where in the column the list goes, and whether it shows at all. its search box finds groups too, above the people: the ones you are in, then the public ones, by this page's rule for the letters (`groupMatches`), each as the same row (`#result-group-<name>`).

## servers

a group other than `bot` is a server. it starts with one channel, `general`. adding another (20 at most) takes the channels power: an owner has it, a role can, a site admin can once they have joined. the same power puts the channels in order and renames them, under `manage`; a new one goes under the last, everyone's until a role keeps it (the roles tab: a role's channels). home shows them under recently offline (`#home-groups`) as squares, yours and the public ones, and a square opens that group. the heading opens `/groups`: a search, `create` to its right, then squares of the groups this login is in, then squares of the public ones it is not. the search waits 200ms after the last letter, then filters those squares and asks `GET /api/search` for people (`#people`). a private group is in yours only.

making one is a dialog (`#groups-make`, `MakeGroupDialog.tsx`), opened by `create` on `/groups` (`#groups-create`) and by `create group` beside home's groups heading (`#home-groups-create`). both show only for a login under the cap on owning (the `owner` servers in `yours` counted against `groups_per_owner`, both from `GET /api/explore`; `mayMakeGroup` in `api.ts`). the dialog is the banner and the picture as they will sit on the group (each the whole picture as the click, `#groups-make-banner-pick` and `#groups-make-icon-pick`, through the crop dialog, and shown in place from then on; the group's colour where there is none yet), the name (`#groups-make-name`) and the badge (`#groups-make-badge`), the about line, eight swatches and the colour box with the badge as chat will show it, public or private, and `make it` (`#groups-make-submit`). that posts the group, then puts the pictures on it (`PUT .../icon`, `.../banner`), and the page is the new room. a picture that did not go up is not a failure: the group is made, and the room offers the pictures again. a refusal from the api (the cap, a taken name) is said in the dialog (`#groups-make-status`).

that page is a server, not a stream. a banner (the picture, or a bar of the group's colour) and the group's picture (or the first letter) sit over a channel list and the chat. for an owner the two are the way to change them: the pointer over the banner or the picture greys it and puts a camera in the middle, the whole picture is the click, and the crop dialog takes it from there (the input is named `change banner` / `change picture`, `add a …` while there is none). a screen with no hover shows a small camera in the corner all the time instead, and no grey. the same two are under `manage` as well, at the top of `about`, for an owner: one row, `group image` over the square on the left and `group banner` over the strip on the right (`#manage-icon-pick`, `#manage-banner-pick`), each the click the same way. nothing on the right for this, and no remove. the chat there is blocks: a larger face, the name on its own row, the text under it, the channel's name in the header (`#channel`). a stream's chat stays one line. whoever in the group is live shows as a tile of the stream they already have, under the channel list on the left (`#group-live`, each tile `#group-live-<name>`), at most four playing. a night with every camera off is still the page: the column is the channels alone, and nothing says the cameras are off. anyone can read, and speaking takes a login that has joined. `general` cannot be deleted. the words `before`, `search`, `by` and `around` are not channel names, because those are the history routes.

the page is two views, and snaps between them (`scroll-snap-type` on `<html>` while the page is up, each view a `snap-start` area): the room, which is where the page opens, the channels, the chat and the roster the height of the window under the title row, a 7px breath between the row and the chat and another under it (`ROOM_HEIGHT`: the window less the row's 4rem and the two 7px; the grid folds the page's foot under itself, so the room's breath ends where the window does), the channels and the roster 7px further in than the chat at the top and the foot, and the top, the banner, the picture, the name and the line about it, a scroll up away. a scroll back down snaps to the room. the landing is on the room's place from the first frame, the skeleton's grid while the room is asked for; the chat's own scroll and the roster's are contained, so the top of a long history does not move the page.

`/groups/<name>` and `/groups/<name>/<channel>` are one page, keyed on the server (`router.tsx`), so a channel is never a different page: the room is asked once (`room-atoms.ts`), with the last 100 lines of every channel, and the chat is one socket for the whole server (`/chat/<name>/*`, `chat.md`) that carries every channel's lines, each with its channel. clicking a channel swaps what the panel shows and asks nothing of anyone; what landed in another channel meanwhile is already there. the list is in the order `manage` put it (`position`; by age until then, `general` first). for the channels power, `add channel` is a row under the last channel (`#group-channel-add`) and opens a dialog (`#channel-make`, `AddChannelDialog` in `ServerRoom.tsx`): the name (`#channel-make-name`, typed to the name rule, with the fault under it when the word is a history route or a channel already) and an optional line about it (`#channel-make-about`), `add it` (`#channel-make-submit`) posts the channel and the page is the new channel; a refusal from the api is said in the dialog (`#channel-make-status`). the row goes at 20 channels, with a line saying so (`#group-channels-full`; `CHANNEL_CAP`, the api's `CHANNELS_PER_SERVER`). a channel kept to roles wears a lock at the right of its row (`KeptMark`; the roles' names its tooltip), here and on the manage dialog's channels tab, for whoever sees it. a member has a bell at the right of each channel's row (`#channel-mute-<name>`, icon only, the word a tooltip), up while the pointer is on the row: a line said in a channel is a row under the other members' bells (`notifications.md`), and a muted one writes none for this login;
```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS channels (
  server text NOT NULL REFERENCES groups(name) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  name text NOT NULL,
  about text NOT NULL DEFAULT '',
  created_by_id uuid REFERENCES streamers(id) ON DELETE SET NULL,
  created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  position integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,   -- the order on the page (migrate-24); created_at, then name, break a tie
  PRIMARY KEY (server, name)
);
```

## not here

- invites and approvals. a private group is unlisted, and a login who has the name can still join.
- renaming a group, or changing its badge or its colour. the about line, the picture and the banner are the edit.
- removing the picture or the banner. an owner changes it for another.
- a rank among roles. powers are a flat set, and reach is by what the shaper holds, not by a ladder.
- deleting one chat line.
- a group's badge on home and on the watch page. those show the `✓`. home's groups list shows the picture.
